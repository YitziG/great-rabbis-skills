# Source Notes

This repository translates documented themes into original agent workflows. It does not reproduce the voice, authority, or complete thought of Rabbi Nachman, the Lubavitcher Rebbe, or Rav Kook.

These notes are maps for further study, not prooftexts for generated advice. They intentionally paraphrase. The linked English pages vary in status: some host primary texts, while others are institutionally maintained explanations or source sheets that identify a primary location.

## Rabbi Nachman of Breslov

### Private reflection

The private, direct practice behind `hitbodedut-brief` is associated here with [Likutei Moharan, Part II 25](https://www.sefaria.org/Likutei_Moharan%2C_Part_II.25.1?lang=bi). The skill keeps the answers off-chat and creates no additional durable artifact by default. It does not claim that a chat service has no retention.

### Good points

The attention to concrete good points behind `nekudah-tovah` and `good-point-retro` is associated here with [Likutei Moharan 282](https://www.sefaria.org/Likutei_Moharan.282.1?lang=bi). The workflow is not forced positivity: evidence of good sits beside an honest constraint or repair.

### Courage, story, renewal, and simplicity

The courage theme behind `narrow-bridge` is associated here with [Likutei Moharan, Part II 48](https://www.sefaria.org/Likutei_Moharan%2C_Part_II.48.2). The skill does not quote the popular English rendering because translations vary.

`story-seed` is associated with Rabbi Nachman's use of story as a mode of attention. Every generated story is labeled as an original parable, never a recovered or adapted tale.

`begin-again`, `simple-path`, `longing-to-plan`, and `simchah-check` are repository-authored workflows grouped around recurring Breslov themes. Their exact procedures are not claimed as source teachings.

## The Lubavitcher Rebbe

### Situated responsibility and action

`mission-from-here` is informed by the Rebbe's recurring treatment of a person's particular place and capacities as consequential. Chabad.org's maintained overview [Impact](https://www.chabad.org/therebbe/article_cdo/aid/61868/jewish/Impact.htm) is an accessible starting point.

`bottom-line-action` and `one-more-good-deed` are informed by the documented insistence that ideas reach practical expression and that a single concrete act matters. See [It's the Deed That Matters](https://www.chabad.org/therebbe/article_cdo/aid/4228511/jewish/Its-The-Deed-That-Matters.htm) and the broader historical overview [13 Ways the Lubavitcher Rebbe Changed the World](https://www.chabad.org/therebbe/article_cdo/aid/3710186/jewish/13-Ways-the-Lubavitcher-Rebbe-Changed-the-World-Forever.htm).

These skills do not translate mitzvah obligations into generic productivity commands. Their steps are modern workflow design.

### Reach, capability, and media

`reach-the-one` is informed by the Rebbe's person-by-person orientation and practical outreach, but its consent, metrics, and campaign-design procedure is original to this repository.

The lamplighter image used by `make-a-lamplighter` belongs to the wider Chabad tradition. The linked account, [The Lamplighter](https://www.chabad.org/parshah/article_cdo/aid/594615/jewish/The-Lamplighter.htm), attributes it to the sixth Lubavitcher Rebbe, Rabbi Yosef Yitzchak Schneersohn—not to the seventh Rebbe. This repository preserves that distinction.

`use-the-medium` is informed by documented use of radio, telephone, satellite, and early networks for constructive reach. See Chabad.org's historical page [The Usage of Technology](https://www.chabad.org/therebbe/article_cdo/aid/62171/jewish/1960-The-Usage-of-Technology.htm). The skill adds contemporary privacy, distortion, and rollback tests; those are repository-authored safeguards.

## Rav Kook

### Return and renewal

`return-to-self` is associated with *Orot HaTeshuvah* 15:10, where return begins with renewed attention to the self and the root of the soul. A [Sefaria source sheet](https://www.sefaria.org/sheets/431212) provides Hebrew and an English rendering for study; verify any quotation against a chosen edition.

`root-cause-return` draws more broadly on *Orot HaTeshuvah*'s treatment of return as a force of restoration and renewed life. [Teshuvah for the Generation of Rebirth](https://ravkooktorah.org/elul_65) identifies several primary sections and provides an interpretive overview. The diagnostic workflow itself is original.

### Four scales and larger wholes

`fourfold-song` is informed by the “Fourfold Song,” identified as *Shemona Kevatzim* 7:112 and collected in *Orot HaKodesh* II. A [Sefaria source sheet](https://voices.sefaria.org/sheets/150060) gathers the passage for study. The skill substitutes “community” for the historically particular “nation” when offering a general decision lens; that adaptation is explicit and should not be mistaken for translation.

`whole-from-parts` is informed by Rav Kook's resistance to treating one partial perspective as the whole. The workflow is not “all views are true”: it explicitly preserves evidence, incompatibility, and moral limits. For a primary location and discussion, see *Orot HaKodesh* I:121 as identified in [this essay on disagreement](https://sapirjournal.org/cancellation/2022/to-err-is-human-to-disagree-jewish/).

`justice-and-mercy` is associated with Rav Kook's treatment of apparently opposing traits as capable of a higher integration. [Creation of the Universe—Twice](https://ravkooktorah.org/breish64) is an interpretive starting point that identifies the justice-and-mercy theme. The governance procedure is original.

### Imagination and form

`responsible-imagination` is informed by Rav Kook's serious treatment of imagination and art as faculties that can disclose and enrich life while requiring ethical formation. See [The Song of Songs](https://ravkooktorah.org/shir61), adapted from an essay later printed in *Olat Re'iyah*. The skill's image-generation and dignity screen are original.

## Quotation and attribution policy

Before adding a direct quotation:

1. Locate the primary work and exact section.
2. Identify the edition and translator.
3. Verify the exact wording in context.
4. Distinguish quotation from paraphrase and later adaptation.
5. Explain why the quotation is necessary to the workflow.

If any step is missing, paraphrase the theme, name the uncertainty, and do not place words in a rabbi's mouth.

## Adding a collection

A future collection needs more than a famous name or visual theme. It needs a documented, distinctive way of attending and acting; several non-overlapping workflows; a reliable source map; the same safety and authorship boundaries; and a parallel, maintainable website surface.
