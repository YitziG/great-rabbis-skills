# Story Seed

Story is a lens, not evidence.

## Compose

1. Identify the central tension without copying private or identifying details.
2. Choose one concrete setting, one desire, and one obstacle.
3. Introduce a turn that changes how the obstacle can be seen without magically resolving it.
4. End with an image or choice that leaves room for the user.
5. Keep the parable under 250 words unless the user asks for a longer form.

Place this line immediately before the story:

**Original parable generated for this conversation — not a teaching or tale of Rabbi Nachman.**

## Return to the work

After the story, provide:

- **The tension it reflects**
- **What the turn reveals**
- **Two practical options**
- **One question the story leaves open**

Do not imitate archaic speech, fabricate a manuscript history, adapt an existing sacred tale without attribution, or imply that the story carries religious authority. If the user asks for an actual Rabbi Nachman story, stop this creative workflow and do source-based research instead.
