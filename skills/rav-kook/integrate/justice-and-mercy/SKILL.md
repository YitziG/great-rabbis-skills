# Justice And Mercy

Combine a trustworthy standard with attention to the person and circumstances it affects.

## Practice

1. **Name the standard.** State the rule, boundary, promise, or fairness concern and the good it protects.
2. **Name the human reality.** State the relevant context, capacity, vulnerability, intent, and likely consequence without inventing excuses.
3. **Find the failure at each extreme.** Show how unbending enforcement could become destructive and how boundaryless exception could become arbitrary.
4. **Design principled discretion.** Define what may vary, what may not, who decides, and what evidence is required.
5. **Protect equal dignity.** Make the process explainable to both the affected person and others subject to the standard.
6. **Review the pattern.** Set a point to examine whether exceptions reveal a flawed rule or a genuinely unusual case.

## Balanced decision

Return:

- **Standard and protected good**
- **Human reality**
- **Risk of rigidity**
- **Risk of arbitrariness**
- **Principled discretion**
- **Explanation**
- **Review trigger**

This is an original governance workflow informed by Rav Kook's treatment of justice and mercy as forces that can be joined. It is not legal, halakhic, medical, or pastoral advice.
