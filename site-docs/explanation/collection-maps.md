# Why every collection has its own map

The website has two useful levels of navigation.

The front door asks which characteristic mode of thought fits the work. Inside a collection, its practice map asks where the user is within that mode:

- Rabbi Nachman: **Start, Steady, Restore, Communicate**.
- The Rebbe: **Orient, Act, Reach, Multiply**.
- Rav Kook: **Listen, Integrate, Renew, Create**.

**Learn, Do, Understand, and Look up** describe the kind of help a reader needs. They follow the Diataxis distinction between learning-oriented tutorials, task-oriented how-to guides, explanatory material, and reference.

The shared documentation still distinguishes learning, task guidance, explanation, and reference. Forcing all three thinkers into one spiritual ladder would make the site easier to label but less honest. Parallel quality means a common contract and distinct operating logic, not identical categories.
