## Overall assessment

The project's most defensible source claim is **original modern workflows inspired by identified themes**. Its source map states that boundary explicitly. The three practices have different levels of documentary proximity: begin-again has an acknowledged broad thematic association; bottom-line-action has clear institutional secondary support for its action theme; justice-and-mercy has a specific interpretive adaptation pointing to a named older source. ## begin-again

**Repository claim:** the six-step restart procedure is author-created and grouped around recurring Breslov themes; SOURCES.md does not present a specific passage as the source of that procedure. Its instructions to preserve artifacts, abandon a failed assumption, choose a testable slice, and define stop conditions are contemporary workflow design. [Skill](https://github.com/YitziG/great-rabbis-skills/blob/4240a0cb231671c7be01863585fae25a0d92ee7d/skills/rabbi-nachman/start/begin-again/SKILL.md)

**Accessible primary-text support for adjacent themes:** Sefaria's text of Likutei Moharan 282:1 discusses finding genuine good in another person and favorable judgment in a framework of repentance. Likutei Moharan II 48:2 discusses persistence in divine service despite repeated falls, encouragement, and the cumulative significance of small movements toward service. These passages support the source summary's good-points and courage themes; the move from spiritual service to project recovery remains an analogy. [Likutei Moharan 282:1](https://www.sefaria.org/Likutei_Moharan.282.1?lang=bi), [Likutei Moharan II 48:2](https://www.sefaria.org/Likutei_Moharan,_Part_II.48.2?lang=bi)

The Sefaria API supplied both Hebrew and English. Its translation metadata identifies Moshe Mykoff, Breslov Research Institute, 1986–2012, volumes 1–11 for part I and 12–15 for part II. This identifies the served translation; I did not collate it against printed editions. No verbatim translation is reproduced here.

**Qualified-reader question:** what primary passage best supports renewal specifically, and how should preserving a spiritual commitment be distinguished from abandoning a failed project approach? The existing citation of 282:1 chiefly concerns judging another person; readers should locate and assess any intended application to self-judgment in the wider teaching rather than assume that subsection proves every modern use.

## bottom-line-action

**Verified support:** Rabbi Yisroel Shmotkin's institutional Chabad.org essay explicitly describes the Rebbe's emphasis on translating ideas into practical programs and the significance of individual mitzvah acts. This is a secondary exposition by an identified author, not a dated transcript of the Rebbe. Its theological setting is divine service and mitzvot, not generic productivity. [It's the Deed That Matters](https://www.chabad.org/therebbe/article_cdo/aid/4228511/jewish/Its-The-Deed-That-Matters.htm)

Mordechai Lightstone's historical overview independently describes practical, limited immediate actions in its sixth section. It is another institutional secondary source, not an independent scholarly assessment. [13 Ways the Lubavitcher Rebbe Changed the World Forever](https://www.chabad.org/therebbe/article_cdo/aid/3710186/jewish/13-Ways-the-Lubavitcher-Rebbe-Changed-the-World-Forever.htm)

**Original adaptation:** owner, start time, measurable receipt, beneficiary, and approval status are the repository's operational design. The inspected sources support the importance of action; they do not establish that particular accountability schema. The skill correctly labels its synthesis. [Skill](https://github.com/YitziG/great-rabbis-skills/blob/4240a0cb231671c7be01863585fae25a0d92ee7d/skills/lubavitcher-rebbe/act/bottom-line-action/SKILL.md)

**Qualified-reader question:** select a dated primary talk or letter, identify its edition, and clarify what is preserved or lost when mitzvah action becomes a general decision aid. The sources do not justify equating the source tradition's value of a deed with a quantified productivity outcome.

## justice-and-mercy

**Verified support:** the cited RavKookTorah essay explicitly combines justice and mercy and identifies itself as adapted from **Shemuot HaRe'iyah 8, Bereishit 5690 (1929)**. It is an interpretive adaptation, not a primary edition checked in this review. That reference is a useful concrete lead for a qualified reader, but its pagination/section convention remains to be verified. [Creation of the Universe—Twice](https://ravkooktorah.org/breish64)

**Contextual limit:** the essay concerns creation, divine attributes, commandments, Eden, and future restoration. Its integration is more demanding than splitting a difference: it describes mercy within justice. Translating this to fair rules, discretion, evidence requirements, explanations, and review triggers is an original governance analogy. The skill names its procedure as original and disclaims legal, halakhic, medical, and pastoral authority. [Skill](https://github.com/YitziG/great-rabbis-skills/blob/4240a0cb231671c7be01863585fae25a0d92ee7d/skills/rav-kook/integrate/justice-and-mercy/SKILL.md)

**Qualified-reader question:** check the cited source in a reliable edition and determine whether this procedural analogy preserves its account of integration, including circumstances where compromise would misrepresent the standard or the person affected.

## Reporting boundary

The desk review supports attribution transparency and identifies plausible thematic connections. It does not establish scholarly fidelity of each adaptation. Machine-rated usefulness and source fidelity remain separate questions: a useful output cannot validate a theological interpretation, and a well-supported theme cannot establish that the workflow improves decisions. Preserve the current source labels; consider the precise primary references and contextual questions in a later, separately reviewed source-note improvement, without changing the frozen pilot.
