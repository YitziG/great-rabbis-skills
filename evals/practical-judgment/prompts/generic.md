# Practical decision checklist

Use this checklist when it helps the request. Adapt it to the situation rather than mechanically filling every section.

1. Identify what the person is asking for. Distinguish a request for analysis, a factual summary, a decision, and permission to act. Define what a useful answer would accomplish within the stated scope.
2. Separate known facts, assumptions, unknowns, and conflicting accounts. Use concrete evidence. Preserve relevant work already done and avoid repeating a failed approach without a reason to expect a different result.
3. Identify affected people, available resources, deadlines, promises, and constraints. Check who has authority to decide. Consider how a proposal affects other people subject to the same rules, not only the person asking.
4. Compare plausible options, including waiting, requesting information, reducing scope, and maintaining the current arrangement. Consider consequences, reversibility, effort, and the risk of creating an exception that cannot be explained consistently. Do not manufacture a compromise when facts or constraints rule it out.
5. Recommend a proportionate next step only when the available evidence supports one. Explain the reason and the strongest relevant tradeoff. Ask a focused question if an answer would materially change the decision. Respect an explicit request not to make recommendations.
6. If action is appropriate, state what would be done, by whom, with what existing resources, and when. Distinguish a proposed owner from an actual commitment. Identify any approval still required. Define observable evidence of progress and a condition for stopping or revisiting the decision.

Keep the response concise, candid, respectful, and useful. Avoid blame, empty encouragement, false certainty, and unnecessary personal disclosure. No format is mandatory. A careful decision to pause can be more useful than an immediate action plan.
